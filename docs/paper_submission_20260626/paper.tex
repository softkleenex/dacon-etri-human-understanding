\begin{abstract}
This paper describes a competition system for the 5th ETRI Human Understanding AI Paper Challenge. The task is to predict seven binary daily targets spanning subjective sleep quality, fatigue, stress, and objective sleep-health guideline adherence from multimodal lifelog data. The confirmed public anchor of the system is ID342, a conservative feature-similarity shrink blend between a known public anchor lineage and an independent feature-similarity source. The workflow prioritizes leaderboard transfer safety over local cross-validation gains: candidate promotion is blocked unless a model improves local validation while also surviving stable-shape, failed-frontier, and public-gap risk checks. The best confirmed public score as of 2026-06-25 is 0.5960832519. Extensive late-stage audits found that weather, AirKorea pollution context, historical ETRI survey/sleep priors, raw temporal representations, missingness-aware subject-state embeddings, and registered material-source recombinations did not open a credible Public 0.58 review path. The resulting system is best characterized as a robust anchor-controlled ensemble with explicit negative-evidence accounting rather than an overfit local-frontier solution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The 5th ETRI Human Understanding AI Paper Challenge asks participants to predict seven daily binary outcomes from smartphone, wearable, sleep, location, activity, light, app-usage, and proximity-derived data. The challenge is framed as an algorithm competition with a required IWETRIAI paper submission through ICTC/EDAS, using the Standard IEEE conference template as a four-page full paper according to the DACON rules \cite{dacon2026rules}. The ICTC paper-submission page and DACON discussion notice record the 2026-06-26 paper deadline \cite{ictc2026submission,dacon2026talkpaper}. The public leaderboard is useful for feedback but is not the final evaluation; therefore, a practical system must avoid chasing local cross-validation artifacts that do not transfer.

Our final confirmed system is intentionally conservative. Instead of presenting a single large model as the solution, we present a risk-controlled modeling workflow centered on a confirmed public anchor. The core contribution is threefold. First, we combine multimodal lifelog predictions through small, target-aware movements from an anchor. Second, we enforce validation gates that explicitly compare candidate movement against known failed public directions. Third, we preserve negative evidence from external and raw-source investigations so that late-stage decisions are reproducible and not driven by leaderboard anxiety.

\section{Task and Data}
The seven targets are Q1 subjective sleep quality relative to each participant's average, Q2 physical fatigue before sleep, Q3 stress before sleep, S1 total sleep time guideline adherence, S2 sleep efficiency guideline adherence, S3 sleep onset latency guideline adherence, and S4 wake after sleep onset guideline adherence. All targets are modeled as binary outcomes, and local evaluation uses the mean multi-target log loss:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{7N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{t=1}^{7}
  \left[y_{it}\log p_{it} + (1-y_{it})\log(1-p_{it})\right].
\end{equation}

The local system uses processed competition features derived from smartphone, wearable, proximity, location, light, screen, activity, app-usage, and sleep-proxy streams. Additional report-only sources were audited: KMA ASOS hourly weather for Daejeon over the competition window, a downloaded AirKorea 2024 hourly archive covering 13 Daejeon stations, historical ETRI lifelog survey/sleep CSVs from 2018--2020, and related ETRI lifelog method papers. These external sources were never promoted into a submission artifact unless they passed the same local and transfer-risk gates; none did.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Anchor-Controlled Candidate Generation}
The confirmed public anchor is ID342, a convex shrink blend between ID178 and ID221:
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{ID342}} = (1-\alpha)p_{\mathrm{ID178}} + \alpha p_{\mathrm{ID221}},
  \quad \alpha=0.50.
\end{equation}
ID178 provided the stable public-anchor lineage, while ID221 supplied an independent feature-similarity source. Later experiments were treated as small target-specific edits of ID342, not as unrestricted global replacements. This design reduced the risk of moving toward overconfident local-frontier predictions that previously failed on the public leaderboard.

\subsection{Validation and Promotion Gates}
The promotion stack used multiple lenses: full out-of-fold log loss, subject-hole splits, leave-one-subject-out style stress checks, test-boundary neighbor holdouts, target-specific local deltas, public-gap proxy estimates, and failed-frontier correlation against known public failures. A candidate was not promoted merely because it improved full local CV. It also had to remain near the stable anchor geometry and avoid alignment with failed submissions such as ID287 and ID329.

This conservative policy emerged from observed local/public divergence. ID287 achieved very low local loss but a poor public score, and ID329 also showed local optimism without public transfer. Therefore, later gates reported both failed-frontier-inclusive and failed-frontier-excluded behavior before any artifact or submission action.

\section{Negative Evidence}
Late-stage work focused on whether materially independent Q3/S3/S4 signal could be found. KMA ASOS weather provided valid historical data but produced only tiny unstable S4 movement near public proxy 0.596006. AirKorea 2024 provided complete Daejeon coverage across the competition window, but its ID342 gate passed zero local, review, or DACON promotion gates, with best proxy 0.596056065. Historical ETRI survey/sleep priors showed coherent Q2 fatigue-to-next-sleep coupling, but stress did not support a simple Q3-from-sleep proxy.

The same pattern held for raw and learned representations. Missingness-aware and self-supervised subject-state runs reached broad raw coverage but produced zero stable downstream passes. MIS-LSTM-style block-sequence feasibility selected only a small S4 signal and failed Q3/S3 transfer. Registered material-source fusion and stable-envelope recombination remained near the current public band or at best around a conservative proxy of 0.566, still far from 0.55. These results support the final decision not to spend additional submissions on same-family micro-signals.

\section{Reproducibility and Safety}
Every promoted or rejected candidate was tied to a notebook lineage, submission CSV status, decision report, and submission-log row when applicable. Raw external caches and public-data API keys were kept outside tracked files. The final deadline policy explicitly froze long Kaggle/heavy runs and low-evidence DACON submissions once the registered routes were closed. The core local records are the DACON three-slot submission result, post-ID342 remaining-route synthesis, external research priority report, AirKorea context gate, historical ETRI sleep/survey prior audit, and deadline operating policy \cite{dacon2026results}.

\section{Conclusion}
The confirmed system did not reach Public 0.55, but it established a reproducible, risk-controlled workflow for a difficult multimodal lifelog task. The main lesson is methodological: local CV alone is not a reliable promotion criterion in this challenge. Stable anchor geometry, failed-frontier avoidance, and target-specific domain gates are necessary to avoid spending submissions on misleading local gains. Future work should focus on genuinely new Q3/S3/S4 mechanisms rather than additional alpha tuning, temperature calibration, or same-family micro-blends.
